# Preregistration: did Colombian oil palm replace forest? Two forest products, two answers?

Written on 2026-11-24, before any forest-loss value was read over an oil palm pixel. Only the dataset catalogues, band names and documented limitations were consulted.

1. **Question.** For oil palm in mainland Colombia planted between 2001 and 2018, what share of the planted area was forest before planting, according to Hansen Global Forest Change and according to JRC Tropical Moist Forest (TMF), and how often do the two products disagree on the same pixel?
2. **Data.** Oil palm: `BIOPAMA/GlobalOilPalm/v1_2` (Descals et al. 2024), pixels with `classification` 1 (industrial) or 2 (smallholder) and `planting_year` from 2001 to 2018. Years 2019 to 2021 are excluded because the dataset documentation says they mostly record replanting and miss young plantations; years up to 2000 are excluded because both forest products start their loss record in 2001. Hansen: `UMD/hansen/global_forest_change_2025_v1_13`. JRC TMF: `projects/JRC/TMF/v1_2025/AnnualChanges`, codes 1 undisturbed, 2 degraded, 3 deforested, 4 regrowth, 5 water, 6 other (checked against the JRC data users guide before running; the script stops if other codes appear). Country outline: `USDOS/LSIB_SIMPLE/2017`, Colombia; departments: `FAO/GAUL/2015/level1`. Everything on the Hansen 30 m grid.
3. **Definitions, fixed now and not tuned.** For a palm pixel with planting year Y:
   - *Forest before planting, Hansen*: `treecover2000` of at least 30 % and `lossyear` between 2001 and Y + 2.
   - *Forest before planting, TMF*: class 1 or 2 in Dec2000, and class 3 (deforested) in at least one year from Dec2001 to Dec(Y + 2).
   - The two-year margin after Y covers the planting-year error stated by the dataset authors (RMSE 2.0 years for industrial plantations).
   - Sensitivity, reported but not used for the decision: Hansen tree cover threshold of 10 % and 50 %.
4. **Estimation.** A simple random sample of 20,000 palm pixels (seed 0) is drawn in Earth Engine and the rules above are applied to it in Python, so they can be unit-tested. Reported, overall and for industrial and smallholder separately: share of palm area that was forest according to Hansen, according to TMF, according to both, and the disagreement (pixels where exactly one product says forest), each with a 95 % interval (2,000 bootstrap resamples, seed 0). The same rules are also computed wall-to-wall in Earth Engine, for the maps only; the wall-to-wall share must fall inside the sample interval, otherwise the run is stopped and the error is found before anything is reported.
5. **Decision rule.** The products are called to *disagree materially* if the disagreement is at least 20 % of the palm area and its 95 % interval lies entirely above 10 %. Otherwise the answer does not depend on the product. Stated expectation: Hansen gives a higher share than TMF, because its tree cover also counts older plantations and secondary vegetation, and the disagreement is material.

**Negative test of the code, before the real run.** Unit tests on synthetic pixels with known histories (forest cleared then planted; pasture planted; old palm cleared and replanted; forest cleared ten years before planting). Each rule is broken on purpose once, and the test must fail naming the wrong case.

**Detail areas for the interactive maps, rule fixed now.** On a 20 km grid, among cells with at least 500 ha of palm planted 2001 to 2018, the two cells with the most palm area where both products say forest, and the two with the largest disagreement in hectares. Only grid cells and imagery are shown: no farm boundaries, owners or company names.

Known limits, stated in advance: neither forest product is ground truth, so this measures disagreement, not accuracy. TMF maps only tropical moist forest, so palm planted on former dry forest (common in the Caribbean region) cannot count as forest in TMF; part of any disagreement is therefore a difference of definition, and the results are also reported by department so that this part can be seen. Planting year is the last bare-land event, so a replanted plantation carries its replanting year. Young, open-canopy and semi-wild palm is missing from the palm map, and smallholder plantations have lower accuracy. The result is not an assessment of compliance with the EU Deforestation Regulation, whose cut-off date (31 December 2020) falls after the period studied.

**Implementation note, 2026-11-25, written before any result was seen.** The two Earth Engine batch tasks over the whole country (stratified sample and wall-to-wall sums) failed with "Computation timed out" after about two hours, without returning anything. Both computations now run on 0.5 degree cells aligned to the Hansen grid (`scripts/tiled.py`). The design is unchanged: the wall-to-wall sums are the same sums added cell by cell, and the sample is still a simple random sample of 20,000 palm pixels, drawn in two steps: a Bernoulli sample with the same probability in every cell (p = 30,000 / total palm pixels, seed 0), then exactly 20,000 of those units drawn at random with seed 0. Input bands with no data under a palm pixel are written as -1 and counted before analysis instead of being dropped silently.
